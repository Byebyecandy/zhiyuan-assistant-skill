# Legal / Release Risk Notes

> This is a practical engineering risk note, not legal advice.

## Short Conclusion

Publishing this repository is lower risk if it is framed as a non-official, non-impersonating methodology skill.

Risk becomes materially higher if the repo:
- uses a real person's name as the product identity,
- says the AI is that person or speaks from that person's perspective,
- copies long passages from books, interviews, videos, or another GitHub repo,
- uses portraits, voice, signatures, QR codes, or platform screenshots without permission,
- implies endorsement, authorization, partnership, or official status.

## Copyright

Copyright protects concrete expression, not abstract ideas or methods. So:

Lower risk:
- summarizing public methods in your own words,
- creating your own prompt workflow,
- linking to sources,
- using short factual notes.

Higher risk:
- copying long quotes from books/articles/videos,
- cloning another repo's README/SKILL text,
- embedding downloaded videos, transcripts, screenshots, or course material,
- using someone else's generated hero image or assets without complying with its license.

The reference repo uses MIT for its own code/text, but you should still avoid copying its prose wholesale. This repo uses an independently written structure and content.

## Name / Personality Rights

Under PRC civil law, natural persons have personality rights including name, portrait, reputation, honor, privacy, and related interests. Deceased persons' name, portrait, reputation, honor, privacy, and remains can also receive civil protection through close relatives' claims.

Practical implications:
- Avoid naming the skill after a real person.
- Avoid "official", "authorized", "same as本人", or "switch to X teacher".
- Avoid generating fake first-person claims, fake endorsements, fake private experiences, or fake positions.
- Avoid using portraits, voice imitation, or face/voice cloning.

## Reputation / Defamation

Risk increases if the repo makes unverifiable claims about a named person, their company, credentials, death, controversy, income, students, or private life.

Use:
- source-tagged short summaries,
- `公开资料显示 / 待核验 / 置信度较低`,
- neutral method labels.

Avoid:
- sensational descriptions,
- moral judgments,
- claiming exact private intent or "真实思维".

## Platform / Video Content

Short-video platforms, articles, and books may have their own terms and rights. Linking to public URLs is safer than rehosting content.

Do not include:
- scraped transcripts,
- downloaded videos,
- watermarked screenshots,
- fan-edited compilations,
- QR codes from a third-party repo.

## Recommended GitHub Positioning

Safer repo names:
- `zhiyuan-assistant-skill`
- `gaokao-application-methods-skill`
- `college-planning-skill-cn`

Riskier repo names:
- `<real-person-name>-skill`
- `<real-person-name>-agent`
- `<real-person-name>-clone`

Recommended disclaimer:

```text
This project is an unofficial education-planning methodology skill. It does not impersonate, represent, or claim endorsement from any real person or institution. All modules are abstractions from publicly available planning styles and are provided for research and educational use.
```

## Release Checklist

- [ ] Repo name is generic, not a real person's name.
- [ ] README says non-official and non-impersonating.
- [ ] No long copied quotes.
- [ ] No portraits, voice, video, QR code, or platform screenshots.
- [ ] No "official/authorized" wording.
- [ ] Sources are linked rather than rehosted.
- [ ] Outputs require policy and admission data verification.
